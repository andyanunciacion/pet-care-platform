## How to run a clinic visit (≈ 20–25 min)

1. **Listen first (10–15 min).** Ask the interview questions in §3 *before* showing anything. Once they've seen the pitch, their answers change.
2. **Show (5 min).** Walk through the deck (§4), or better, show **their own clinic page** on your phone once the staging site exists.
3. **Ask (2–3 min).** Ask for a small, concrete commitment (§8). Then ask who else you should talk to.
4. **Write notes within the hour** using the template in §10.

Tips:
- Talk in whatever mix of English, Filipino, or Kapampangan they're comfortable with.
- Ask about **past behavior** ("last week, how many…"), not opinions about the future ("would you use…?"). People are polite about hypotheticals.
- Never invent numbers, users, or partner clinics. It's fine to say "we're launching in Angeles and talking to clinics first."

## 1. One-liner

**PAW helps pet owners in Angeles find your clinic, see your real services and hours, and contact you in one tap, for free.**

## 2. 30-second pitch

> Most pet owners here find a vet through Google Maps or by asking in Facebook groups. The info they find is often outdated. People show up when you're closed, or call to ask whether you offer a service you've had for years.
>
> PAW is a directory built specifically for pets. Every clinic gets a free page showing its services, price ranges, the animals it treats, and its hours, with an "open now" status and the date the hours were last confirmed. Owners can call, message you on Messenger, or get directions in one tap.
>
> We set up the page for you. All we ask is that you check it and confirm your hours once a month, which takes less than a minute.

## 3. Interview questions (ask BEFORE pitching)

**How clients find them today**
- How do most *new* clients find your clinic?
- Do you have a Facebook page or website? Who updates it, and how often?
- Have you checked what Google Maps shows for your clinic? Is it correct?

**The pain**
- On a typical day, how many calls or messages are just "Open ba kayo?", prices, or "Do you do ___?"
- In the last month, did anyone come in while you were closed, or for a service you don't offer?
- When your hours change (holidays, the vet is away), how do you let clients know?
- Which services do clients *not know* you offer?

**Marketing and money**
- What do you currently spend to get clients (FB boosts, flyers, tarpaulins, promos)? What worked?
- Who handles your phone and Messenger? How busy are they?

**After showing the demo** (reaction questions)
- What's wrong or missing on this page?
- Would you claim it? What would stop you?
- To get a *Verified* badge we'd ask for the vet's PRC license number and your business permit. Is that okay?
- How would you rather keep it updated: log in yourselves, or just message us and we update it?
- For a monthly "are your hours still correct?" reminder, what would you actually answer: SMS, email, Messenger, or Viber?
- What would you want to know about the people who view your page?
- If there were extra paid features later, which would be worth paying for? *(Don't quote a price. Ask what they'd expect.)*
- Who else should I talk to? Another clinic, or a vet friend?

## 6. How it works — clinic workflow

*(Subject to your feedback. This is the current plan.)*

**1. We list you.** PAW creates a basic page from public information and by confirming details with you. It shows as *"Unclaimed"*.

**2. You claim it.**
1. Tap *"Claim this listing"* on your page.
2. Verify the clinic's listed phone number with a code sent by SMS or a call.
3. Enter the PRC license number of the vet in charge and upload your business/Mayor's permit.
4. PAW reviews it (usually within a few days) → **Verified** badge.

**3. Keep it fresh.**
- Update services, prices, photos, and hours anytime, including holiday closures.
- Once a month you get a reminder. One tap on "Still correct" updates the *"last confirmed"* date that owners see.
- If an owner reports wrong info, you approve or reject it.

**4. See results.** A monthly summary of how many owners viewed your page, called, messaged, or asked for directions.

## 7. Handling objections (FAQ)

| They say… | You answer… |
|---|---|
| "We already have a Facebook page." | Great, PAW links to it. But owners can't search Facebook by service, animal, or "open now", and FB pages rarely show up when someone Googles "vet near me that does kapon". PAW sends them to you. |
| "We're already on Google Maps." | PAW doesn't replace it; our directions button opens Google Maps or Waze. The difference is that anyone can edit Google's info, and it doesn't show services, prices, animals treated, or when the hours were last confirmed. |
| "We don't have time." | We set up the page for you. Keeping it updated is one tap a month. If you prefer, just message us changes and we'll update it. |
| "What's the catch? How much?" | The basic listing is free, always. Later there'll be optional paid extras, but nothing is required, and paid placements will always be labeled "Sponsored". |
| "What if people post bad reviews?" | Reviews aren't part of the launch. When they come, they'll be from real accounts only, moderated, and you'll have a public right of reply. |
| "What do you do with our documents?" | They're used only to verify you, stored privately, and handled under the Data Privacy Act. |
| "Can we be removed?" | Yes. Tell us and we'll hide the listing. *(Confirm this policy before launch.)* |
| "We don't want to show prices." | That's fine. Use "call for price", or show a rough range only. |
| "Who are you?" | *[Your story: local connection, why you're building this, how to reach you.]* |

## 8. The "ask" (end of every visit)

Pick **one** concrete commitment. Something small is fine:
- [ ] "Can I create your page now and send you the link to check?"
- [ ] "When we launch, will you claim it? Can I get the best number or Messenger to reach you?"
- [ ] "Can I leave this QR standee so clients can see your hours on PAW?" *(once the site is live)*
- [ ] "Who's another clinic I should talk to?"
